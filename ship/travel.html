Travel Guide

Three real ports worth the trip, with what to see, when to go and how to get there.

Rincón: surf town on the west coast

A laid-back corner of Puerto Rico known for big winter waves, humpback whales offshore and some of the best sunsets in the Caribbean.

See and do

  • Surf Domes, Maria's and Steps beaches. Local surf schools teach beginners.
  • Punta Higüera Lighthouse Park: gazebos, viewing binoculars and the sunset spot in town.
  • Tres Palmas Marine Reserve: elkhorn coral and reef fish when the sea is calm.
  • Thursday Art Walk at the main plaza, with music, crafts and bomba dancing.
  • Bosque Comunitario La Armonía: boardwalks through a community forest.

When to go

  • Surf season: October to February, when the northwest swells arrive.
  • Whales: humpbacks from about November to April. January to March is the best stretch.
  • Calm water: late spring and summer are better for snorkeling and swimming.
  • Check tides and conditions. Beaches that look calm can turn dangerous in the winter swell season.

Getting there

  • Fly into Aguadilla (BQN), about an hour's drive, or San Juan (SJU), about 2.5–3 hours.
  • Rent a car. Most beaches need one.
  • U.S. dollars, and no passport needed for U.S. citizens.

Three days in Rincón

Day 1Surf lesson in the morning, lighthouse at sunset.
Day 2Snorkel Tres Palmas if it's calm, then a whale-watch boat in season.
Day 3Forest boardwalk, beach-hopping, Art Walk if it's Thursday.

Valparaíso: the painted port

A UNESCO World Heritage port city built across steep hills, with colorful houses, street art and old funiculars climbing above the bay.

See and do

  • Cerro Alegre and Cerro Concepción: cobbled streets, murals, galleries and cafés.
  • Ride the ascensores: the Reina Victoria and El Peral funiculars give big views of the bay.
  • La Sebastiana: poet Pablo Neruda's house museum, high on the hill.
  • Plaza Sotomayor and the port down below.
  • Next door, Viña del Mar has beaches and a seaside promenade.

When to go

  • Good all year. Summer, December to March, has the best weather. Chile's seasons are the reverse of the U.S.
  • New Year's Eve fireworks over the bay are famous.
  • At night, stick to well-lit, busy streets or take a taxi between the hills and downtown.

Getting there

  • About 120 km from Santiago: roughly 1.5 hours by car on Ruta 68, or about 2 hours by bus.
  • Fly into Santiago (SCL), the nearest airport.
  • U.S. citizens don't need a visa for stays of up to 90 days (see the Santiago tab).

Two days in Valpo

Day 1Ascensores, Cerro Alegre and Concepción, and sunset over the bay.
Day 2La Sebastiana in the morning, then Viña del Mar's beaches.

Santiago: the capital under the Andes

Chile's big city, with snow-capped mountains on the horizon, hilltop parks, lively neighborhoods and wine country an hour away.

See and do

  • Cerro San Cristóbal: ride the funicular up for the view over the city and the Andes.
  • Cerro Santa Lucía: a small hill garden right downtown.
  • Plaza de Armas, La Moneda Palace and Mercado Central for history and seafood.
  • Lastarria and Bellavista neighborhoods, including La Chascona, another Neruda house.
  • Day trips to the Maipo and Casablanca wine valleys, or skiing in the Andes in winter.

When to go

  • Spring (September to November) and fall (March to May) are mild.
  • Winter (June to September) brings ski season in the nearby Andes.
  • Summer, December to February, is hot and dry.

Getting there

  • Fly into Santiago (SCL).
  • U.S. citizens: no visa for tourist stays of up to 90 days, and no entry fee. Keep the entry slip you get on arrival, since hotels use it for the tax exemption on lodging.
  • Declare any food, plant or animal products on the customs form.
  • Currency is the Chilean peso.

Three days in Santiago

Day 1Plaza de Armas, La Moneda, lunch at Mercado Central.
Day 2Cerro San Cristóbal, then Bellavista and La Chascona.
Day 3Wine valley day trip, or head to Valparaíso.
